About

I'm a robotics engineer focused on perception, state estimation, and SLAM, currently pursuing a Master of Science in Robotic Systems Development (MRSD) at Carnegie Mellon University. I'm most interested in keeping robots localized where the usual sensors stop being reliable: GPS-denied structures, dust-filled underground mines, and visually degraded scenes.

This summer at Komatsu, I built IMU-free 4D radar odometry for underground mines where dust degrades LiDAR, along with a PCA-based ego filter for LiDAR point clouds and a real-time camera calibration pipeline in ROS 2. At CMU, I work on Flying Hand, an aerial manipulator for non-destructive testing, where I integrated cuVSLAM for GPS-denied localization on a Jetson Orin Nano and built a nested visual-servoing and force-control stack. For my SLAM course project, I stress-tested ORB-SLAM3 across 1,760 trials to map how image corruptions break monocular tracking.

Before CMU, I earned a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering from IIT Ropar, where I built multi-robot UGV–UAV mapping systems with ROS 2 and PX4. Long term, I want to deploy autonomy in high-stakes settings such as search and rescue, disaster relief, and environmental monitoring, where safety and reliability matter most.

I'm seeking full-time roles in perception, localization, and state estimation after I graduate in May 2027.

Monocular Visual SLAM Robustness Study

How image corruptions break ORB-SLAM3 tracking, indoors vs. outdoors

SLAM Course Project · Dr. Michael Kaess, CMU · Mar – May 2026

Ran 1,760 controlled ORB-SLAM3 trials across 22 EuRoC and KITTI sequences under Gaussian noise, shot noise, and motion blur at five severity levels, mapping which corruptions cause outright tracking failure versus ATE degradation. Uncovered a sharp indoor–outdoor gap: outdoor tracking fell below 40% success at severity 2, while indoor sequences held above 60% through severity 3, with outdoor ATE roughly 100× larger.

Autonomous Mushroom Harvesting

Grasp, twist, and pull: sim-to-real harvesting of oyster mushrooms on a Franka arm

Robotic Manipulation · MuJoCo & Franka Hardware

Specialty mushrooms are labor-intensive to harvest and easily damaged. This project frames harvesting oyster mushrooms from vertical indoor racks as a sequence of constrained manipulation actions (lateral grasp, twist to detach, and transfer) and runs one planning and execution pipeline consistently in MuJoCo and on a physical Franka arm. The real robot successfully harvested five mushrooms from a vertical rack setup.

  • Used a parameterized twist-and-pull strategy to bridge sim-to-real differences in detachment.
